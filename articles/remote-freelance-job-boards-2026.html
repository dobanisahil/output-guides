Best Places to Find Remote Freelance Work in 2026 (Beyond Upwork and Fiverr)

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Upwork and Fiverr aren't the only options, and for some skill sets, they're not even the best ones. Here's where else to actually look.

Niche job boards (often better than general platforms)

For specific skills - writing, design, dev, customer support, QA - a niche job board usually has less competition per listing than a general marketplace, since it's not open to literally every freelancer worldwide. Worth searching "[your skill] + remote job board" rather than defaulting straight to Upwork.

Direct outreach beats all of them for experienced professionals

If you already have 5+ years in a specific field (operations, QA, training, support, etc.), cold outreach to small companies that need that skill part-time or on contract often converts better than competing in an open marketplace against hundreds of generalist bids. Slower to start, but higher-value work once it lands.

LinkedIn, used correctly

Not as a resume-dump, but posting specific, concrete examples of work you've done (a process you fixed, a result you drove) attracts inbound interest from people hiring for exactly that skill. This works especially well for operations, QA, and training-adjacent roles where "show me what you've actually built" matters more than a portfolio link.

Platforms that are more hype than help

Be skeptical of any platform charging an upfront fee to "access better clients," or promising guaranteed placement. Legitimate freelance platforms make money from a cut of what you earn, not from charging you to join.

How to actually pick

If you want volume and don't mind competing on price early: general marketplaces (Upwork, Fiverr, PeoplePerHour - see our full comparison). If you have real experience in a specific field: direct outreach and LinkedIn presence, slower to start but better-paying once it does.
